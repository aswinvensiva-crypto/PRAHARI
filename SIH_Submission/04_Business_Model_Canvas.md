## 1. CUSTOMER SEGMENTS (who we serve)

Primary government customers (paying)
- Who procures: State Tourism Departments and Tourism Development Corporations, District Administrations and District Tourism Promotion Councils, and the Ministry of Tourism (for national dashboards and Swadesh Darshan 2.0 and SASCI monitoring). Hill and pilgrimage development authorities are also buyers.
- Budget cycles and procurement: annual budgets from April to March. Purchases go through GeM (Government e-Marketplace), innovation-challenge follow-ups, or pilot work orders under central scheme funds.
- Decision-makers and influencers: Principal Secretary or Director (Tourism), District Collector or Deputy Commissioner, the state IT department and State e-Governance Mission Team, and NIC.
- Pain points: no real-time data, reactive crowd control, court-mandated capacity limits, and pressure to show outcomes from scheme investments.
- Size: 36 states and union territories, 780+ districts, and 57 Swadesh Darshan 2.0 destinations plus the Budget's 50 priority destinations. Willingness to pay: high for proven, outcome-linked tools. Adoption: pilot within 6 months, state contracts in 12 to 24 months.

End-user beneficiaries (free or freemium)
- Travellers: domestic travellers (about 2.5 billion visits a year, mostly mobile-first, rising use of UPI) and foreign tourists (about 9.5 million arrivals a year). Current workaround: guessing from social media and getting stuck in crowds.
- Hotel, homestay and MSME hosts: small hotels, lodges, dharamshalas and homestays, many of them in rural and hill areas, run by families, women-led enterprises and self-help groups. They use WhatsApp daily but not complex dashboards. Current workarounds: walk-ins, agents, or OTAs at a 15% to 25% commission.
- Influence on design: WhatsApp-first, local-language-first, and a zero joining fee.

Secondary stakeholders
- Implementation partners: system integrators, state IT agencies and NIC.
- ONDC network participants: buyer apps and gateways.
- Training partners: Institutes of Hotel Management, IITTM, and skill councils (THSC).
- Regulators: the Ministry of Tourism (NIDHI), state disaster management authorities, and the Data Protection Board.

## 2. VALUE PROPOSITIONS (the unique value we deliver)

For government buyers
- Cost savings: early warning and pre-positioning reduce emergency rescue, overtime and congestion costs. One avoided peak-day crisis can justify the annual licence.
- Efficiency gains: weekly destination reports in about 10 minutes instead of about 2 days of manual compilation, and 7 to 14 days of advance visibility of peaks.
- Compliance: a transparent carrying-capacity methodology that supports court and NGT directions, full DPDP Act 2023 compliance, and alignment with GIGW 3.0 and CERT-In requirements.
- Political and citizen value: smoother long weekends, visible safety measures, and more income for local residents, which improves citizen and visitor satisfaction.
- Digital transformation: it adds intelligence to existing e-pass, NIDHI and portal systems without replacing them.

For end users
- Travellers: a better trip with less traffic and fewer queues, verified stays, fair prices, disaster alerts and one-tap SOS, available 24x7 in their own language.
- Hosts: listing in under 10 minutes, single-digit distribution fees instead of 15% to 25%, weekly demand forecasts and fair price suggestions, and more off-season bookings.

Unique differentiators (why PRAHARI is more than 30% better)
- A closed loop of predict, measure and redistribute. Competitors do only one of these.
- A capacity-constrained twin rebalancing algorithm that prevents overflow cascades.
- A Dynamic Carrying Capacity Index that turns one-time studies into a daily number.
- Deep integration with India's digital public infrastructure (ONDC, Bhashini, UPI, NIDHI, SACHET, 112 and 1363).
- Proprietary assets: a destination-twin knowledge graph and a model trained on Indian tourism seasonality.

## 3. CHANNELS (how we reach and deliver)

Government sales channels
- Pilot proof-of-concept with one district through SIH follow-up support and the Ministry of Tourism and state innovation cells.
- Listing on GeM, and tender participation through system-integrator partners for large states.
- State startup policies and innovation challenges (for example Startup India, MeitY Startup Hub, state startup missions).

End-user reach channels
- Traveller PWA (no install needed), plus discovery inside ONDC buyer apps and state tourism portals through a widget or API.
- WhatsApp bot for hosts, and onboarding camps with district tourism offices, NRLM self-help groups and hotel associations.
- Integration with e-pass systems: applicants without a slot are shown twin destinations.

Marketing and awareness
- Tourism conferences (for example the Ministry of Tourism's national conferences and state travel marts), ONDC and Digital India events.
- Pilot case studies and a public impact dashboard.
- Social media travel creators promoting the "undiscovered twins" of famous destinations, and state tourism social media handles.
- Traditional media and government communication channels during peak seasons ("Check PRAHARI before you travel").

## 4. CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS (how we interact)

Government relationship management
- A dedicated state success manager, with a monthly review with tourism officials and a quarterly outcome report.
- Custom training for district officers and helpdesk staff.
- Technical support with a guaranteed SLA (99.5% uptime, and response to critical issues within 1 hour during peak seasons).
- Co-innovation: new features co-designed with states, such as pilgrimage slot management.

End-user engagement
- Self-service help in the PWA and a WhatsApp FAQ bot, with multilingual support in Hindi, English and regional languages through Bhashini.
- A community of host champions in each valley, who give peer support.
- In-app feedback and ratings after every stay, and quarterly host satisfaction surveys.

Long-term retention
- Continuous feature updates, personalised demand insights, and green points loyalty rewards for travellers who choose less-crowded places and times.
- Proactive monitoring of the platform, with alerts to hosts before an issue affects bookings.

## 5. REVENUE STREAMS (how we generate income; all figures indicative)

Primary revenue sources
- Government SaaS licence for PRAHARI Drishti with Pulse and Balance, tiered by coverage. Indicative prices: about Rs 25 to 40 lakh a year per district cluster, about Rs 1 to 3 crore a year for a state-wide licence, and a multi-year discount.
- Implementation and integration services (e-pass, portal, NIDHI and state data integration), charged as one-time fees.
- Training and capacity-building programmes for officials and hosts.
- Annual maintenance and support contracts.

Host and industry revenue
- A freemium model for hosts. Listing and basic insights are free. A small ONDC transaction fee (single-digit percentage, well below OTA levels). An optional premium analytics plan (for example Rs 299 to Rs 999 a month) for pricing optimisation and multi-property management.
- Anonymised, aggregated insight reports for hotel chains, investors, airlines and tourism boards. These never contain personal data.

Pricing strategies
- Tiered pricing by the number of destinations covered, plus performance-linked bonuses tied to KPIs (forecast accuracy, overflow shifted).
- Volume discounts for multi-district or multi-state contracts. Subscription billing gives predictable recurring revenue.

Alternative revenue opportunities
- White-label licensing to private destination managers (hill resorts, theme parks, heritage trusts).
- Consulting for destination master plans and carrying-capacity studies.
- International expansion to countries with similar overtourism and MSME challenges (Nepal, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia).

## Impact and ROI snapshot (illustrative)

- A peak weekend with 1 lakh visitors spending Rs 4,000 each puts Rs 40 crore at stake. Retaining 5% gives about Rs 2 crore a weekend.
- Two such weekends a season retain about Rs 4 crore, which is 10 or more times the pilot cost, before counting host commission savings, off-season occupancy gains and lower emergency costs.
- Host savings: on a Rs 2,000 room-night, moving from a 15% to 25% commission to a single-digit fee saves Rs 200 to Rs 400 per night.
