Predictive Routing & AI for Hotels, Attractions, Regions & Itineraries
AI Demand Forecasting & Crowd Rebalancing to Boost Hotels, Homestays and Travel in India
PREDICT · PROTECT · REDISTRIBUTE

PRAHARI forecasts where and when tourists will go, compares that forecast with each destination's live carrying capacity, and redirects surplus demand to nearby “twin” destinations and small hotels that have room. It is built on ONDC, Bhashini and UPI, so overcrowding becomes income for local tourism businesses instead of a crisis.

1 · The problem is mismatch, not shortage

Too many tourists in too few places on too few days, while nearby hotels and homestays stay empty.

Five pain points we solve

  • Overtourism at hotspots: hours-long traffic jams, water and waste stress, crowd-safety risk on long weekends and festivals.
  • Empty rooms nearby: MSME stays sit half-empty for most of the year with strong seasonality.
  • Digital exclusion: small hosts are unlisted or dependent on high-commission OTAs, and face language barriers.
  • No foresight for officials: tourist data arrives months late, so there is no forecast for next weekend.
  • Trust & safety gaps: overcharging, fake listings, and tourists stranded by monsoon disasters.

Why existing solutions fail

  • OTAs sell rooms · Portals inform · Permits restrict · Map apps react
  • No one connects demand forecasts with carrying capacity and redistribution.

Who is affected

TravellersLost time, surge prices, safety risk, poor experience
Hotels & MSMEsOff-season losses, commission drain, no demand visibility
CommunitiesCongestion, water & waste stress, rising resentment (Doxey's Irridex)

4 · Core Algorithms & Technology Choices

Input → Processing → Output: proven techniques combined in a new way, with every tool justified.

PRAHARI
Technical Depth

A. PULSE: multi-horizon demand forecasting

  • Features: lags (1, 7, 14, 28, 364 days), rolling statistics, long-weekend flags, school-vacation overlap, festival & event intensity, weather anomalies, search momentum, origin-city demand.
  • Models: LightGBM (fast, SHAP-explainable) + Temporal Fusion Transformer (known-future inputs), with ETS/ARIMA fallbacks for sparse series.
  • Consistency: MinT reconciliation across attraction → destination → district → state. Cold start: transfer from a cluster of similar destinations.

B. D-CCI: dynamic carrying capacity & status

PCC = (A / a) × Rf // area per visitor × daily rotations
RCC = PCC × Π(1 − Cfᵢ) // weather, landslide, roads, parking, water
ECC = RCC × MC // management capacity (staff, services)
D-CCI(d,t) = ECC with Cfᵢ recomputed daily
LR = Forecast(d,t) / D-CCI(d,t) → G <0.8 · A 0.8–1 · R >1 · Black = hazard

Based on Cifuentes (1992), extended from a one-time study into a live daily index.

C. BALANCE: capacity-constrained twin rebalancing

  • Twin candidates: cosine similarity of attribute and description embeddings, with a travel-time penalty and spare-capacity filter.
  • Allocation: min-cost flow (OR-Tools) sends overflow nudges without pushing any twin above 0.8 × its own D-CCI, which prevents overflow cascades.
  • Incentives: a contextual Thompson-sampling bandit learns which nudge (discount, free experience, priority slot, green points) works for which segment, within budget.
  • Fairness: exposure is rotated among eligible small stays so large properties are not favoured.

Market opportunity

  • B2G: 36 states and UTs, 780+ districts; 57 Swadesh Darshan 2.0 destinations; 40 SASCI iconic-centre projects; the Budget 2025-26 plan to develop 50 top destinations.
  • Hosts: the large, unorganised base of small hotels, lodges and homestays, with branded inventory under 2 lakh rooms. Homestays have MUDRA-loan support in Budget 2025-26.
  • Travellers: ~2.5 billion domestic visits and ~9.5 million foreign arrivals a year.

Beachhead market

  • Hill-station and pilgrimage clusters where courts and regulators already require capacity controls: Kullu-Manali, the Nilgiris (Ooty, Kodaikanal), Mussoorie, Darjeeling and the Char Dham routes.
  • These buyers already feel the pain and have budgets, so they are the fastest path to a paid pilot.

Target-user research plan

  • Hosts (n ≥ 50): occupancy by month, OTA use and commission, WhatsApp usage, language, willingness to pay.
  • Travellers (n ≥ 200): would you switch to a quieter twin? Which incentive works? What is your trust threshold?
  • Officials (5–8 interviews): DTOs and state tourism IT on data gaps, e-pass pain points and procurement route.
  • Results feed the incentive-bandit priors and UX decisions before the pilot.

Validation metrics

  • Share of travellers who accept a twin suggestion (target ≥ 10%).
  • Host activation: listing completed within 10 minutes (target ≥ 70%).
  • Officials: weekly dashboard use and forecast-versus-actual accuracy.

Defensible advantages

  • D-CCI scoring method and capacity-aware rebalancer (patent potential).
  • Destination-twin knowledge graph for India.
  • Deep integration with ONDC, Bhashini, NIDHI, SACHET, 112 and 1363.
  • Data network effect: every host and every season improves forecasts and recommendations.
  • Open standards, so states adopt without lock-in, which makes PRAHARI a candidate for tourism Digital Public Infrastructure.

Why now

  • ONDC, Bhashini and UPI are mature enough to build on.
  • Budget 2025-26 destination development and SASCI investments need outcome tracking.
  • Climate extremes make early warning a necessity.
